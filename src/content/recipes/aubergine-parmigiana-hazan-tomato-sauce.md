Aubergine, tomato sauce, cheese, oven. Hard to argue with.

The useful move here is using leftover [Marcella Hazan-style tomato sauce](/recipes/marcella-hazan-tomato-sauce/) instead of passata. It is already cooked down, buttery and sweet, so the bake tastes like it has had more time than it really has.

## Ingredients

- 2 aubergines
- Olive oil
- 2 teaspoons dried oregano
- 2 small red onions, finely sliced
- 500g [Marcella Hazan-style tomato sauce](/recipes/marcella-hazan-tomato-sauce/)
- 200g mature Cheddar, grated
- Salt and black pepper

### To serve

- Crisp lettuce
- Lemon juice or vinegar
- Extra virgin olive oil

## Method

### 1. Prepare the aubergines

Heat the oven to 180C.

Trim the aubergines and slice them lengthways into pieces about 1cm thick.

Put them in a large bowl with olive oil, salt, black pepper and 1 teaspoon dried oregano. Toss well so the slices are lightly coated.

### 2. Brown the aubergines

Heat a large frying pan over a medium-high heat.

Fry the aubergine slices for about 3 minutes on each side, until they have taken on some colour and started to soften.

Work in batches if needed. Do not crowd the pan.

Set the aubergine aside.

### 3. Cook the onions

Add a little more oil to the same pan.

Fry the red onions for 4-5 minutes, until softened and starting to colour.

Set them aside with the aubergines.

### 4. Layer

Spoon a little tomato sauce into the base of an ovenproof dish or ovenproof frying pan.

Add a layer of aubergine, then some red onion, more tomato sauce, black pepper, a little oregano and a handful of Cheddar.

Repeat until everything is used up, finishing with tomato sauce and a generous layer of Cheddar.

### 5. Bake

Bake for 25-30 minutes, until the sauce is bubbling and the top is golden.

Leave it for 5-10 minutes before serving. It holds together better once it has calmed down.

### 6. Serve

Toss crisp lettuce with lemon juice or vinegar, a little extra virgin olive oil, salt and black pepper.

Serve alongside the parmigiana.

## Why this works

Aubergine needs colour before it goes into the oven. If it goes in raw, it stays spongy and disappointing.

The Hazan-style tomato sauce is doing the passata job, but better: tomato already rounded out with butter and onion. That means the bake can be simple without tasting thin.

Cheddar is not traditional, but it melts, browns and brings sharpness. It works.

## No-faff notes

- Leftover tomato sauce is perfect here. If it is very thick, loosen it with a splash of water before layering.
- If your sauce still has the onion halves in it, lift them out before using it here.
- Keep the aubergine slices around 1cm thick. Too thin and they vanish; too thick and they take ages to soften.
- The lettuce is not decoration. You want something crisp and sharp next to all that soft aubergine and cheese.

## No-faff swaps

- No Hazan-style tomato sauce? Use passata, but season it well and add a small knob of butter or a drizzle of olive oil.
- No Cheddar? Use mozzarella, Parmesan, pecorino or a mix.
- No red onions? Use regular onions or shallots.
- Want it more substantial? Eat with bread, potatoes or a green salad.
