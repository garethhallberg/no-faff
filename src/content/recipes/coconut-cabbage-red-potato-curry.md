This is what to make when you have an onion, a bit of cabbage, a potato, maybe a carrot, and a can of coconut milk.

The potato makes it filling, the carrot adds sweetness, the cabbage makes it cheap, and the coconut milk turns the whole thing into a proper curry rather than boiled vegetables with spices.

## Ingredients

- 1 onion, sliced
- 2 red potatoes, cut into small chunks
- 1 carrot, cut into small chunks or half-moons, optional
- 1/4 white cabbage, finely sliced
- 1 can coconut milk
- 1 tablespoon oil
- Salt and black pepper
- A handful of small tomatoes, blitzed, optional

### Spices

Use what you have:

- 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
- 1/2-1 teaspoon chilli flakes or chilli powder
- 1 teaspoon curry powder
- 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander
- A pinch of garam masala, to finish

### Optional extras

- 1-2 garlic cloves, chopped
- A thumb of ginger, grated
- Lemon, lime or vinegar
- A pinch of sugar
- Chickpeas, lentils or tofu

## Method

### 1. Cook the onion

Warm the oil in a pan over a medium heat.

Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 8-10 minutes, until soft and starting to turn golden.

### 2. Add the spices

Add any garlic or ginger, if using, and cook for 1 minute.

Add your spices and stir for 30 seconds.

Do not let them burn. If the pan looks dry, add a splash of water.

### 3. Cook down the tomatoes

If using tomatoes, add them now.

Cook for 5-8 minutes, stirring now and then, until they have thickened into the spiced onion and the oil starts to show at the edges.

This is where the extra flavour happens. Do not just warm them through and hope.

### 4. Add the potatoes and carrot

Add the red potatoes and carrot, if using, and stir until they are coated in the spiced onion.

Pour in the coconut milk, then add half a can of water.

Season with salt and black pepper.

### 5. Simmer

Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 12-15 minutes, until the potatoes and carrot are nearly tender.

Stir now and then so nothing catches.

### 6. Add the cabbage

Add the sliced cabbage and stir it into the sauce.

Simmer for another 8-10 minutes, until the cabbage has softened and the potatoes are cooked through.

If the sauce gets too thick, add a splash more water.

### 7. Balance

Taste before serving.

If it is flat, add salt. If it feels heavy, add lemon, lime or a splash of vinegar. If the chilli or spices taste harsh, add a tiny pinch of sugar.

Serve with rice, [chapatti](/recipes/chapatti/) or [yoghurt flatbreads](/recipes/yoghurt-flatbreads/).

## Why this works

Potatoes thicken the coconut sauce as they cook. Carrot brings sweetness. Cabbage goes soft without disappearing. The onion and spices keep it from tasting like coconut soup.

## Make it immense

- Add chickpeas with the cabbage for more protein.
- Finish with coriander, spring onion or crispy onions.
- Add a spoonful of chilli oil at the table.
- Serve with [lemon rice](/recipes/lemon-rice/) if you want it brighter.
- Blitzed tomatoes cooked down before the coconut milk make it deeper and less sweet.

## No-faff swaps

- No red potatoes? Any potato works. Cut floury potatoes a little bigger so they do not collapse completely.
- No carrot? Leave it out, or use a bit more potato.
- No curry powder? Use cumin, turmeric and chilli.
- No tomatoes? Leave them out. The curry still works.
- No coconut milk? Use stock and finish with yoghurt, but keep the heat gentle once the yoghurt goes in.
- No cabbage? Use spinach, kale, cauliflower or broccoli.
- Want it thicker? Simmer uncovered for a few minutes.
