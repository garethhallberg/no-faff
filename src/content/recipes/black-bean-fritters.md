A tin of black beans, an onion, a carrot or two. Not much, but cooked slowly and fried properly, these turn into something genuinely good.

The carrot and onion base is the secret. It gives sweetness, depth, and a texture that holds everything together.

## Ingredients

- 1 can black beans, drained well
- 1 white onion, finely diced
- 1–2 carrots, finely diced
- 2–3 cloves of garlic, minced
- A handful of breadcrumbs
- Oil for frying
- Salt and black pepper

**Optional:**

- Cumin, coriander seed, smoked paprika, chilli flakes
- A tiny splash of soy sauce
- A squeeze of lemon or a dash of vinegar

## Method

### 1. Build the base

Warm a glug of oil in a frying pan over a low-medium heat.

Add the diced onion and carrot. Cook slowly for 10 to 15 minutes.

You want softened, sweet, and just starting to catch at the edges. Not rushing this is the point.

Add the garlic and cook for another minute. If using spices, add them here and let them bloom in the oil for 30 seconds.

Take off the heat and leave to cool slightly.

### 2. Blitz the beans

Drain the beans well — excess water will make the mixture hard to handle.

Pulse briefly in a food processor, or mash with a fork. You want a rough paste with some texture, not a smooth purée.

### 3. Combine

Mix the beans with the onion and carrot base. Add the breadcrumbs, season with salt and pepper, and stir through a small splash of soy sauce if using.

### 4. Judge the texture

This matters more than any exact measurement.

You want a mixture that is moist enough to hold together but dry enough to form into patties. If it feels too wet, add more breadcrumbs a tablespoon at a time. Too dry, add a drizzle of oil or the smallest splash of water.

Shape into patties. Make them thinner than feels right — thin fritters crisp properly and cook through without that hot-mush problem you get in thick ones.

### 5. Fry

Heat a little oil in a frying pan over a medium heat.

Lay the fritters in and leave them alone. Resist the urge to move them early — they are fragile until the crust sets.

After 3 to 4 minutes, check the underside. When it is properly golden and firm, flip carefully. Cook the other side for another 3 minutes or so.

## Serve with

- Lemon yoghurt
- Chilli sauce
- A quick cabbage salad
- Homemade coleslaw
- Or tuck them inside flatbreads with any of the above

## No-faff notes

- No food processor? A fork works. You just want some beans mashed, not all of them.
- These reheat well in a dry pan — far better than a microwave, which undoes the crust.
- Leftovers in a flatbread the next day are excellent.
