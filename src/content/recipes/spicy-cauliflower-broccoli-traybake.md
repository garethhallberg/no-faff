This is the supermarket bag of cauliflower and broccoli turned into actual dinner.

The spice paste gets into all the rough edges, the oven makes them crisp, and lemon at the end stops everything feeling heavy. Add chickpeas and yoghurt and nobody can call it just some veg.

## Ingredients

- 1 bag cauliflower and broccoli florets
- 1 red onion, cut into wedges or thick slices
- 2-3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 tablespoon tomato puree
- 2 garlic cloves, grated or finely chopped
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
- 1/2-1 teaspoon chilli flakes
- Salt and black pepper
- Juice of 1/2 lemon

### Optional but good

- 1 can chickpeas, drained
- A handful of cashews, peanuts or walnuts
- Fresh coriander

### For the yoghurt finish

- 2 tablespoons plain yoghurt
- A squeeze of lemon
- A pinch of salt
- A pinch of cumin or chilli flakes

## Method

### 1. Heat the oven

210°C fan.

### 2. Build the tray

Put the cauliflower, broccoli and red onion into a large roasting tray.

Add the chickpeas too, if using.

Keep everything in one fairly shallow layer. Use two trays if it is crowded, or the vegetables will steam instead of crisping.

### 3. Mix the spice paste

Mix the olive oil, tomato puree, garlic, cumin, paprika, turmeric, chilli flakes, salt and black pepper in a small bowl.

Add a tiny splash of water to loosen it into a coating paste.

### 4. Coat and roast

Toss the vegetables thoroughly in the spice paste. Get it properly into the nooks of the cauliflower.

Roast for 25-35 minutes, turning once halfway through, until the cauliflower is browned and the broccoli is crisp at the edges.

Add nuts for the final 5 minutes if using.

### 5. Finish

Squeeze over the lemon juice.

Taste. Add more salt if it is flat, more lemon if it feels heavy, or a tiny touch of sweetness if the tomato and chilli taste harsh.

Scatter coriander over the top if you have it.

### 6. Make the yoghurt finish

Mix the yoghurt with the lemon, salt and cumin or chilli.

Drizzle it over the hot tray, or serve it alongside so it stays cool and sharp.

## Why this works

Cauliflower takes the spice paste into every crevice and colours properly in a hot oven. Broccoli gets crispy tips. Tomato puree gives the coating body, and yoghurt or lemon gives the heat something cool and sharp to bounce off.

## Make it a meal

- Add chickpeas to the tray and serve over [lemon rice](/recipes/lemon-rice/) or plain basmati.
- Eat it with [chapatti](/recipes/chapatti/) or [yoghurt flatbreads](/recipes/yoghurt-flatbreads/).
- Spoon over yoghurt or add crumbled feta-style cheese at the end.
- Pile leftovers into a flatbread the next day.

## No-faff swaps

- No mixed bag? Use just cauliflower, just broccoli, or whatever florets are cheapest.
- No smoked paprika? Ordinary paprika is fine, or use a little extra cumin and chilli.
- No yoghurt? Finish with extra lemon and olive oil instead.
- No chickpeas? Add tofu cubes, paneer or cooked lentils at the end.
- Want a sweeter finish? Add 1 teaspoon mango chutney, honey or sugar to the yoghurt or spice paste.
