Cheap, old-school and exactly what you want when the weather is miserable.

Potato, onion, mustard and proper sharp cheese under a puff pastry lid. Nothing clever. Nothing missing.

## Ingredients

- 750g potatoes, peeled and cut into small chunks
- 2 large onions, finely sliced
- 40g butter
- 1 tablespoon plain flour
- 200ml milk
- 1 teaspoon English mustard or Dijon mustard
- 150g mature cheddar or Manchego-style cheese, grated
- 1 sheet ready-rolled puff pastry
- A little milk or beaten egg, for brushing
- Salt and black pepper

## Method

### 1. Cook the potatoes

Heat the oven to 200°C fan.

Boil the potatoes in salted water for 8-10 minutes, until almost tender but not falling apart.

Drain well and leave them to steam dry.

### 2. Cook the onions

Melt the butter in a large pan.

Add the onions with a pinch of salt and cook gently for 12-15 minutes, until soft, sweet and starting to colour.

Do not rush them. Pale onion makes a disappointing pie.

### 3. Make it creamy

Stir the flour into the onions and cook for 1 minute.

Gradually stir in the milk until you have a thick sauce.

Add the mustard and plenty of black pepper.

### 4. Add the cheese and potatoes

Take the pan off the heat and stir in most of the cheese, keeping a small handful back.

Fold in the potatoes gently. Taste for salt and pepper.

Tip everything into a pie dish and scatter the remaining cheese over the top.

### 5. Put the lid on

Lay the pastry over the dish. Trim the excess and press the edges down.

Cut a small slit in the middle so steam can escape, then brush the top with milk or egg.

### 6. Bake

Bake for 25-30 minutes, until the pastry is puffed and deeply golden and the filling is bubbling at the edges.

Leave it for 5 minutes before serving. The filling will be dangerously hot and it tastes better once it settles.

## Why this works

The potatoes give the pie body, the onions make it sweet, the mustard stops the cheese feeling heavy, and the pastry gives you the crisp top that makes a plain filling feel properly substantial.

## Make it immense

- Serve with peas and [no-faff vegetarian gravy](/recipes/no-faff-vegetarian-gravy/).
- Add a little thyme or chopped parsley to the filling.
- Use a mix of cheddar and Manchego-style cheese for more bite.
- A spoonful of wholegrain mustard gives it a bit more texture.

## No-faff swaps

- No pastry? Top it with buttery mashed potato and bake until golden.
- No cheddar? Use Red Leicester, Lancashire, Cheshire, Gruyere or Manchego-style cheese.
- No milk? Use vegetable stock for a lighter filling and add a bit more cheese.
- Want more vegetables? Add cooked leeks, peas or wilted spinach.
- Want a crisper mash lid? Rough it up with a fork and scatter cheese over before baking.
