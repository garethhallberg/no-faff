Creamy without being heavy, spiced without needing a cupboard full of ingredients.

The important bit is warming the yoghurt gently before it goes into the pan. It makes a smooth, clinging sauce instead of a split one.

## Ingredients

- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 2 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 tablespoon tomato puree
- 1 fresh tomato, blitzed, or about 150g passata or tinned tomato
- 1 can chickpeas, including the liquid
- 4-5 tablespoons plain full-fat yoghurt
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
- 1/2-1 teaspoon chilli powder
- 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek, ideally kasuri methi
- Salt and black pepper

### Optional extras

- Lemon juice
- A pinch of sugar
- Fresh coriander

## Method

### 1. Cook the onion

Heat the oil in a pan and cook the onion for 8-10 minutes, until soft and just starting to turn golden.

### 2. Add the garlic and tomato

Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute.

Stir in the tomato puree and cook for another minute, letting it darken slightly.

Add the blitzed tomato, a good grind of black pepper and a small pinch of salt.

Cover and cook for 5-8 minutes, until the tomato has softened into the onion and the mixture looks thicker and glossier.

### 3. Temper the yoghurt

In a bowl, mix the yoghurt with the cumin, turmeric, chilli powder and fenugreek.

Take the pan off the heat, or turn it right down. Stir a spoonful of the hot tomato mixture into the yoghurt, then another spoonful, to warm it gently.

Stir the yoghurt mixture back into the pan.

### 4. Cook the sauce

Cook gently, covered, for 5 minutes.

Keep the heat low and do not let it boil hard, or the yoghurt may split.

### 5. Add the chickpeas

Add the chickpeas and about half of the liquid from the can.

Cover and simmer gently for 10 minutes.

Remove the lid, add more chickpea liquid if you want a looser sauce, then simmer uncovered for another 5-10 minutes, until the sauce is thick and clinging to the chickpeas.

### 6. Finish

Taste and adjust with more salt, a squeeze of lemon, extra chilli, or a tiny pinch of sugar if the tomato tastes sharp.

Serve with rice, flatbread, toast or a spoonful of extra yoghurt on top.

## No-faff notes

- The chickpea liquid loosens the sauce first, then helps it thicken as it reduces.
- Give the chickpeas 15-20 minutes in the sauce. They absorb far more flavour than they do in a quick heat-through.
- Fresh coriander over the top is good if you have it.

## No-faff swaps

- No fresh tomato? Use passata or tinned tomatoes.
- No kasuri methi? Leave it out, or finish with a little chopped coriander.
- No chickpeas? Butter beans or cannellini beans work well in the same sauce.
- No full-fat yoghurt? Greek yoghurt works; loosen it with a splash of water before tempering.
- Want it vegan? Use an unsweetened plant yoghurt that will stand up to gentle heating.
