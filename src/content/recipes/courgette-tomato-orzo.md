I'd chopped some vine tomatoes the noght beofre to have with some salad. All I'd done is sprinkle some salt on them. Turns out that's a good thing as they get better being left with a little salt on them.

Add onion, garlic, courgette, chilli and orzo. Fantastic lunch.

## Ingredients

- 150g orzo or other tiny pasta
- A bowl of chopped tomatoes, salted in advance
- 1 onion, chopped
- 1 courgette, cut into matchsticks
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- A pinch of chilli flakes
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- Salt and black pepper

## Method

### 1. Cook the pasta

Bring a pan of well-salted water to the boil.

Cook the orzo until just tender, then drain. Keep a little pasta water in case you need to loosen everything later.

### 2. Start the onion

Warm the olive oil in a frying pan over a medium heat.

Add the onion with a pinch of salt and saute until soft and starting to turn sweet.

### 3. Add courgette and garlic

Add the courgette matchsticks and stir them through the onion.

After a couple of minutes, add the sliced garlic. Cook until the courgette is softening but has not collapsed.

### 4. Add tomatoes and chilli

Add the salted tomatoes and a pinch of chilli flakes.

Cook for about 10 minutes, until the tomatoes have slumped and everything looks saucy.

### 5. Stir in the orzo

Add the cooked orzo and give it all a good stir.

Add a splash of pasta water if it looks tight. Taste for salt, chilli and black pepper.

## Why this works

Salting the tomatoes early pulls out their juice and seasons them properly. That juice becomes the sauce. The onion brings sweetness, the courgette softens into it, and the garlic and chilli stop it all being too polite.

## No-faff notes

- If your tomatoes are not already salted, chop them first and sprinkle with salt while you prep everything else.
- Do not brown the garlic. Add it after the courgette so it perfumes the pan without turning bitter.
- Tiny pasta is the point. Orzo, risoni, ditalini or little soup pasta will all work.
- A drizzle of extra virgin olive oil at the end is a good idea.
