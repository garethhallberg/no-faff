Roast tomatoes. Mix filling. Assemble raw pastry. Bake once.

The cottage cheese turns into a soft, savoury filling, the tomatoes concentrate first, and the almond-cashew finish gives the whole thing crunch.

## Ingredients

- 1 pack ready-rolled pastry
- 450-500g cottage cheese
- 1 egg
- 35-40g Parmesan, finely grated
- 300-350g cherry tomatoes, halved
- 1 bunch spring onions
- A small bunch of chives, chopped
- 20g almonds, roughly chopped
- 20g cashews, roughly chopped
- 1/2-1 fresh chilli, finely chopped
- 1 small garlic clove, finely grated, or a good pinch of garlic powder
- Olive oil
- Salt and black pepper

## Method

### 1. Roast the tomatoes and spring onions

Heat the oven to 200C, or 180C fan.

Line a baking tray with baking paper.

Put the cherry tomatoes cut-side up on the tray. Add the spring onions alongside them.

Drizzle lightly with olive oil, then season with salt and black pepper.

Roast for 15-20 minutes, until the tomatoes have softened and started to concentrate. You do not want them dried out.

### 2. Make the chilli-garlic oil

Mix the chopped chilli with 1 1/2 tablespoons olive oil, the garlic and a little pinch of salt.

This is not a proper chilli crisp. It is just the chilli-garlic-oil layer that sits under the cheese and makes the tart better and it works.

### 3. Toast the nuts

Toast the almonds and cashews in a dry frying pan for 3-4 minutes, tossing frequently.

Keep them in rough pieces. You want crunch, not dust.

### 4. Make the filling

Drain off any obvious liquid from the cottage cheese.

Blitz it briefly if you want something closer to ricotta, but do not worry about getting it completely smooth.

Mix the cottage cheese with the egg, Parmesan, chives, half the toasted nuts and about half of the roasted spring onions, chopped.

Add plenty of black pepper and a small pinch of salt. Go easy: the Parmesan is already doing work.

### 5. Assemble without pre-baking

Turn the oven down to 180C, or 170C fan.

Line your tart tin with the pastry. If the pastry comes rolled in baking paper, use that underneath: it makes the tart easier to lift out later and saves scrubbing the tin.

Press the pastry into the edges, then trim or fold whatever needs sorting.

Prick the base a few times with a fork.

Brush or spoon the chilli-garlic oil thinly over the pastry base.

Spread over the cottage cheese filling.

Top with the roasted tomatoes and the remaining roasted spring onions. Press the tomatoes slightly into the cheese rather than leaving them perched on top.

### 6. Bake

Bake for about 30 minutes.

Start checking at 25 minutes. Ready-rolled pastry does not need an hour of punishment.

You are looking for golden pastry edges, a firm-ish centre, a little colour on the cheese, and tomatoes that look properly roasted.

If it needs another 5 minutes, give it another 5.

### 7. Finish

Leave it alone for 10 minutes before cutting.

Scatter over the remaining toasted almonds and cashews, a little more chopped chive and black pepper.

Finish with the tiniest drizzle of good olive oil.

## Why this works

Roasting the tomatoes first gets rid of some water and concentrates their flavour, so the tart tastes like tomatoes rather than damp pastry.

The cottage cheese brings body without becoming heavy. Egg helps it set. Parmesan gives salt and savoury depth.

The chilli-garlic oil underneath is the quiet move. It seasons the base of the tart and gives heat without needing a separate sauce.

## No-faff notes

- No pre-baking. The filling is not wet enough to justify the extra faff.
- Baking paper under the pastry is useful if your tin is not loose-bottomed, or if you want to lift the tart out cleanly.
- Drain the cottage cheese if it looks watery.
- Use the nuts in two places: some inside the filling, some on top after baking.
- Let the tart sit before cutting. Ten minutes makes a big difference.

## No-faff swaps

- No chives? Use parsley, basil or the green tops from the spring onions.
- No cashews? Use all almonds, walnuts or pumpkin seeds.
- No fresh chilli? Use chilli flakes in the oil.
- No Parmesan? Use pecorino or a mature hard cheese.
- Want it richer? Add a little grated cheddar to the filling.
