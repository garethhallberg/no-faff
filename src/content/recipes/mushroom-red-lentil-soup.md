A simple lentil soup, but with the mushroom bit done properly.

Brown the mushrooms first. Do not just sweat them into the soup and wonder why it tastes grey. Red lentils make the broth creamy by themselves, so keep the soup rustic and leave the mushrooms in pieces.

## Ingredients

- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 250-300g mushrooms, sliced
- 120-150g red lentils, rinsed
- 1 litre vegetable stock
- 2 garlic cloves, sliced or crushed
- 1 teaspoon thyme
- 1 tablespoon olive oil, plus more if needed
- Small splash of soy sauce
- Black pepper
- Salt
- Lemon juice, to finish

### Optional extras

- 1 carrot, finely chopped
- 1 celery stick, finely chopped
- A few extra browned mushrooms, kept back for the top

## Method

### 1. Brown the mushrooms

Heat the olive oil in a large pan over a fairly high heat.

Add the mushrooms in a single layer if you can. If the pan is crowded, cook them in batches.

Leave them alone long enough to brown, then stir and keep cooking until they have given up their water and started to take on proper colour.

Salt them lightly towards the end.

Lift the mushrooms out into a bowl. Keep back a small handful of the best browned ones for serving if you like.

### 2. Soften the onion

Turn the heat down to medium.

Add the onion to the same pan with a little more oil if it needs it.

Cook for 8-10 minutes, until soft and sweet. Add the carrot and celery here too, if using.

### 3. Add garlic and thyme

Add the garlic and thyme.

Cook for 1 minute, until the garlic smells good but has not browned.

### 4. Add lentils and stock

Add the rinsed red lentils and vegetable stock.

Bring to a simmer and cook for about 15 minutes, stirring now and then.

The lentils should be softening and starting to thicken the liquid.

### 5. Return the mushrooms

Add the browned mushrooms back to the pan.

Simmer for another 5-10 minutes, until the lentils are completely soft and the soup is creamy but still rustic.

### 6. Finish

Add black pepper, a small splash of soy sauce and a squeeze of lemon.

Taste for salt. Serve with the reserved browned mushrooms on top.

## Why this works

Mushrooms need heat before they need liquid. Browning them first concentrates their flavour and gives you something savoury to bite into.

Red lentils collapse enough to thicken the soup without blending. That means creamy broth, soft lentils and proper pieces of mushroom.

Soy sauce is not there to make it taste of soy sauce. It backs up the mushrooms. Lemon at the end stops the whole bowl feeling flat.

## No-faff notes

- Do not crowd the mushrooms if you can avoid it. Crowded mushrooms steam.
- Add the mushrooms back late so they keep some texture.
- Keep this rustic. A full blend would waste the browned mushroom work.
- It thickens as it sits. Loosen leftovers with water or stock.

## No-faff swaps

- No fresh garlic? Use garlic powder with the thyme.
- No thyme? Rosemary works, but use less.
- No soy sauce? Use a little Marmite, miso or mushroom ketchup.
- Want it richer? Finish with olive oil.
- Want it sharper? Add more lemon.
