This is not a shopping-list recipe. It is what to do when you have rice, an onion and odds and ends that need turning into dinner.

Use one or two vegetables, one spice direction, and something fresh or crunchy at the end. Do not empty every jar in the cupboard into it.

## Ingredients

- 160g basmati rice
- 320ml cold water or vegetable stock
- 1 onion, finely sliced
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 tablespoon butter, optional
- 1-2 cloves garlic, chopped, optional
- Around 250g vegetables, chopped into small pieces
- Salt and black pepper

### Choose some flavour

- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
- 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander
- A pinch of chilli flakes
- 1 bay leaf
- A squeeze of lemon

### To finish

- A handful of toasted nuts or seeds
- Chopped parsley, coriander, mint or dill
- Plain yoghurt, to serve

## Method

### 1. Start the onion

Warm the olive oil and butter, if using, in a wide pan with a lid.

Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 8-10 minutes, until soft and starting to turn golden.

### 2. Add spices and vegetables

Add cumin seeds first, if using, and let them sizzle for a few seconds.

Add the garlic, any other spices and your vegetables.

Cook for 3-5 minutes, until everything has started to soften and is coated in the oniony oil.

### 3. Add the rice

Stir in the basmati rice and turn it through the vegetables for a minute.

Add the cold water or stock, a pinch of salt and a bay leaf if using.

### 4. Cook

Bring to the boil.

As soon as it boils, put the lid on, turn the heat right down and cook for 10 minutes.

Do not stir it while it cooks.

### 5. Rest and finish

Turn off the heat and leave the pan with the lid on for 5 minutes.

Fluff through with a fork. Add lemon, herbs and toasted nuts or seeds if you have them.

Serve with a spoonful of yoghurt on the side.

## What works well

- Peas, sweetcorn or spinach: stir through at the end so they stay bright.
- Carrot, pepper, courgette or small broccoli pieces: cook with the onion before adding the rice.
- Mushrooms: fry until their water has evaporated before the rice goes in.
- Cooked chickpeas, lentils or beans: fold through at the end to warm them up.

## Why this works

The rice absorbs the flavoured cooking liquid while the vegetables steam gently around it. Toasted nuts or seeds give crunch, herbs give freshness, and yoghurt makes a plain pan of rice feel like an actual meal.

## No-faff swaps

- No basmati? Long-grain rice works; check the packet timing if it usually needs longer.
- No fresh vegetables? Frozen peas, sweetcorn or spinach are ideal here.
- No nuts? Use pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds or sesame seeds.
- No yoghurt? Finish with lemon and olive oil instead.
- Want it richer? Add a fried egg, halloumi or crispy tofu on top.
