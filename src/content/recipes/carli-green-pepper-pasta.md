This is a pepper pasta, not a tomato pasta.

The tomatoes are there to disappear into the soft green peppers and help the oil cling to the pasta. Do not turn it into tomato sauce.

## Ingredients

- 180-200g pasta
- 5-6 Carli green peppers, fairly thinly sliced
- 1 echalion shallot, fairly thinly sliced
- A few tomatoes, chopped
- 2-3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 garlic clove, finely chopped, or a little garlic powder
- A pinch of chilli flakes
- Pecorino or Parmesan, grated
- Salt and black pepper
- Extra virgin olive oil, to finish

## Method

### 1. Slow-fry the peppers

Warm the olive oil in a large frying pan over a medium-low heat.

Add the sliced Carli peppers and shallot with a good pinch of salt.

Cook for 10-12 minutes, stirring now and then, until the peppers are really soft and starting to colour.

### 2. Add chilli, garlic and tomatoes

Add the chilli flakes and garlic.

Cook for a minute, then add the chopped tomatoes.

Cook for another 5-8 minutes. The tomatoes should disappear into the peppers rather than become a separate sauce.

### 3. Cook the pasta

While the peppers finish, cook the pasta in well-salted water until al dente.

Keep plenty of pasta water before draining.

### 4. Toss

Add the pasta directly to the peppers with a good splash of pasta water.

Toss over the heat until the oil, tomato juices and starchy water emulsify and coat everything.

Add more pasta water if it needs loosening.

### 5. Finish

Take the pan off the heat.

Add plenty of pecorino or Parmesan, black pepper and another small glug of good olive oil.

Toss again and serve.

## Why this works

Slow-fried green peppers go sweet and soft, but keep a slight bitterness that makes the pasta interesting.

The tomatoes bring just enough juice and acidity to help the sauce come together. Pasta water does the rest. Cheese goes in off the heat so it melts into the coating instead of clumping.

## No-faff notes

- Keep the peppers moving gently, but do not rush them. This recipe is mostly waiting for them to soften properly.
- The tomatoes should be background. A few is enough.
- Pecorino is sharper and saltier. Parmesan is rounder. Both work.
- Short pasta, spaghetti, linguine or tagliatelle are all fine.

## No-faff swaps

- No Carli peppers? Use any long green peppers.
- No echalion shallot? Use a small onion or a couple of regular shallots.
- No fresh garlic? Garlic powder is fine here.
- Want it richer? Add a knob of butter with the cheese.
- Want it brighter? Add a small squeeze of lemon at the end.
