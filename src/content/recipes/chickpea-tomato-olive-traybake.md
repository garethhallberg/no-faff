Tip things into a roasting tray, turn them over in paprika oil, put them in the oven.

The tomatoes collapse into a rough sauce, the chickpeas catch at the edges, and the olives do the salty work. Bread underneath or yoghurt on top and dinner is dealt with.

## Ingredients

- 1 can chickpeas, drained
- 250-300g cherry tomatoes, or chopped fresh tomatoes
- 1 red onion, cut into wedges
- 2-3 garlic cloves, sliced or lightly crushed
- A handful of olives
- 1 tablespoon tomato puree
- 2-3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika or ordinary paprika
- Chilli flakes, to taste
- Salt and black pepper
- 1/2 lemon, for juice or thin slices
- A splash of red wine vinegar, optional

### To serve

- Yoghurt, cottage cheese or crumbled feta-style cheese
- Parsley, dill or basil
- Toasted walnuts
- Bread, rice, couscous or flatbreads

## Method

### 1. Heat the oven

200°C fan.

### 2. Fill the tray

Put the chickpeas, tomatoes, onion wedges, garlic and olives into a roasting tray.

Keep it in a fairly shallow layer so the chickpeas roast rather than steam.

### 3. Mix the coating

In a small bowl, mix the tomato puree with the olive oil, paprika, chilli flakes, salt, pepper and a squeeze of lemon.

If it looks too thick to toss through the tray, loosen it with a tablespoon of water.

### 4. Roast

Pour the coating over everything and toss well.

Add a few thin lemon slices too, if you like.

Roast for 30-40 minutes, stirring once halfway through, until the tomatoes have collapsed, the onion is soft and the chickpeas are catching at the edges.

### 5. Finish

Taste. Add more lemon, a splash of vinegar or more salt if it needs waking up.

If the tomatoes taste particularly sharp, a tiny pinch of sugar will sort them out.

Serve hot, warm or at room temperature.

## Why this works

The tomatoes give up their juice in the oven and mix with the tomato puree and oil into a sauce. The exposed chickpeas get toasted on the outside while staying soft inside. Olives and lemon stop it being just a tray of sweet roast vegetables.

## Make it immense

- Roast chunks of courgette, pepper, mushroom or aubergine alongside it.
- Add parboiled potatoes before roasting and it becomes a full tray dinner.
- Spoon seared tofu, cottage cheese, yoghurt or crumbled feta-style cheese over it at the end.
- Stir through cooked lentils or couscous while the tray is still warm.

## No-faff serving ideas

- Spoon into [yoghurt flatbreads](/recipes/yoghurt-flatbreads/) with yoghurt and chilli oil.
- Serve over [lemon rice](/recipes/lemon-rice/) or plain basmati.
- Pile onto thick toast and finish with olive oil.
- Eat with coleslaw or [tomato salad](/recipes/tomato-salad/) and call it lunch.

## No-faff swaps

- No cherry tomatoes? Any fresh tomatoes work. Chop them roughly first.
- No olives? Use capers, or finish with a little feta-style cheese for saltiness.
- No paprika? Use cumin, oregano or just black pepper and chilli flakes.
- No chickpeas? Butter beans or cannellini beans work, but will be softer rather than crisp.
