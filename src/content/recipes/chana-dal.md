Yellow chana dal is split chickpeas. It is nuttier and more textured than red lentils, and it takes longer to cook, but there is nothing difficult about it.

Make it fairly thick, with plenty of ginger and cumin, then eat it with rice, chapatti, roasted cauliflower, yoghurt at the table, or all of the above.

## Ingredients

- 200g yellow chana dal
- 700-800ml water, plus more as needed
- 1 teaspoon turmeric
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 2-3 garlic cloves, sliced or crushed
- 1 thumb of ginger, grated
- 1 fresh chilli, chopped, or 1/2 teaspoon chilli flakes
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- 1/2 teaspoon garam masala
- 1 tin tomatoes, or 2-3 fresh tomatoes, chopped
- 1-2 tablespoons oil
- Salt
- Lemon or lime, to finish

## Method

### 1. Rinse the dal

Rinse the chana dal well until the water runs mostly clear.

Soak it for an hour or two if you have time. This speeds things up, but it is not compulsory.

### 2. Cook the dal

Put the dal in a saucepan with 700ml water and the turmeric.

Bring to the boil, skim off any foam, then lower to a gentle simmer.

Cook partially covered for 35-50 minutes, adding more water if it needs it.

You want the grains completely tender, with some starting to collapse. Chana dal keeps more texture than red lentils, so do not expect it to vanish into a puree.

### 3. Cook the onion

While the dal cooks, make the flavour base.

Heat the oil in a frying pan over a medium heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 8-10 minutes, until properly softened.

### 4. Add cumin, garlic, ginger and chilli

Add the cumin seeds and let them sizzle for 30 seconds.

Add the garlic, ginger and chilli. Cook for another minute, until everything smells good.

### 5. Add coriander and tomatoes

Stir in the ground coriander, then add the tomatoes.

Cook until the tomatoes have reduced into a thick sauce. This should take 8-10 minutes with tinned tomatoes, a little less with good fresh ones.

### 6. Combine

Stir the tomato base into the cooked dal.

Add salt and enough extra water to get the consistency you like.

Simmer everything together for another 10 minutes.

### 7. Finish

Stir in the garam masala right at the end.

Finish with lemon or lime juice.

## Why this works

Cooking the dal first means it gets properly tender before the acidic tomatoes go in. The tomato base cooks separately, so the onion sweetens, the cumin blooms, and the tomatoes reduce instead of tasting watery.

Chana dal has more bite than red lentils. That is the point. You want soft grains with some texture left, not a completely smooth soup.

## Make it immense

- Serve with [spicy cauliflower and broccoli traybake](/recipes/spicy-cauliflower-broccoli-traybake/) and basmati rice.
- Spoon plain yoghurt over it at the table.
- Finish with extra ginger fried briefly in oil.
- Add a handful of coriander leaves if you have them.

## No-faff notes

- Soaking is useful, not essential. Unsoaked dal just takes longer.
- If it catches on the bottom, turn the heat down and add more water.
- If it tastes flat, it needs salt or lemon.
- Make it thicker for scooping with bread, looser for rice.

## No-faff swaps

- No fresh chilli? Use chilli flakes.
- No fresh tomatoes? Tinned tomatoes are fine.
- No ground coriander? Use a little curry powder.
- No garam masala? Leave it out and add more cumin.
- Want it richer? Use ghee instead of oil, but it will no longer be vegan.
