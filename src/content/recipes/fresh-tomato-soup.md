Tinned tomatoes will do this all winter. This is the version for when the tomatoes are actually good.

A grated carrot does the job cream or sugar would otherwise do. It disappears in the first ten minutes and leaves behind sweetness and body.

## Ingredients

- 1kg ripe tomatoes
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 1 large carrot, coarsely grated
- 2 cloves garlic, sliced
- 500ml vegetable stock
- 250ml water
- Extra virgin olive oil
- Salt and black pepper
- A handful of basil leaves
- 1 tablespoon tomato puree, if the tomatoes are disappointing

## Method

### 1. Skin the tomatoes

Cut a shallow cone out of the stem end of each tomato to take the core out. It is woody and it never softens.

Score a small cross in the base.

Drop them into boiling water for 20 to 30 seconds, then straight into cold water. The skins slip off in your fingers.

Quarter them. Keep the seeds and jelly, that is where the flavour is.

### 2. Onion and carrot

Warm a good glug of olive oil in a large pan.

Add the onion and grated carrot with a pinch of salt. Cook gently for about 10 minutes.

Soft and sweet, no colour. The carrot should be collapsing, not frying.

### 3. Garlic

Add the garlic and cook for a minute, until it smells good.

If you are using tomato puree, add it now and fry it out for another minute. That darkens it and takes the tinny edge off.

### 4. Tomatoes and liquid

Add the tomatoes and stir to coat.

Pour in the stock and the water. Just enough to cover.

Season with salt and black pepper.

### 5. Simmer

Bring to a gentle simmer, lid off, and cook for about 30 minutes.

The tomatoes should collapse completely and the liquid should reduce a little.

### 6. Blend

Take the pan off the heat and blitz with a stick blender until smooth.

Taste for salt. It takes more than you think.

### 7. Finish

Tear the basil in now, after blending, not before.

Finish with olive oil.

## Why this works

Half stock and half water keeps the tomato in front. All stock and you are eating stock.

Skinning matters because skins do not break down. Blend them and you get red flecks all the way through.

Basil goes in at the end because blitzing it into hot soup turns it muddy and dulls the flavour.

## No-faff notes

- Throw the basil stalks into the pan for the last ten minutes and fish them out before blending. Free depth.
- If the tomatoes are not up to much, halve them, oil and salt them, and roast at 200C for 30 minutes first. Deeper soup.
- It thickens overnight. Loosen with a splash of water when reheating.

## No-faff swaps

- No stick blender? Crush it against the side of the pan with a potato masher. Chop the onion small if you are going this way, or it stays chunky.
- Want it velvety? Push it through a sieve after blending, or use a jug blender.
- No basil? A strip of orange peel in with the stock, out before blending.
- Want it richer? A spoonful of creme fraiche stirred in at the end.
- Want it hotter? A pinch of chilli flakes in with the garlic.
