Mushrooms, properly fried, then coated in a thick tomato masala.

This is the dry-ish sort of curry you eat with chapatti, roti, naan, rice, dal, or whatever else is on the table. The mushrooms should be coated, not swimming.

The squeeze of lemon at the end is not decoration. It makes the whole thing snap into focus.

## Ingredients

- 500g button or chestnut mushrooms, wiped clean and halved or quartered
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 thumb of ginger, crushed or finely grated
- 1-2 green chillies, slit or sliced
- 2 large tomatoes, pureed or very finely chopped
- 1 teaspoon turmeric
- 1 1/2 teaspoons ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon chilli powder, or less to taste
- 1 teaspoon garam masala
- 1 teaspoon kasuri methi, crushed between your fingers
- Salt
- Fresh coriander, to finish
- Lemon juice, to finish properly

## Method

### 1. Prepare the mushrooms

Do not wash the mushrooms under the tap. Wipe them clean with a damp cloth or kitchen paper.

Halve them, or quarter any large ones.

### 2. Temper the seeds

Heat the oil in a large frying pan or wok over a medium-high heat.

Add the cumin seeds and mustard seeds. Let them sizzle and splutter for about 30 seconds.

### 3. Cook the aromatics

Add the onion with a pinch of salt.

Cook for 5-6 minutes, until soft and translucent. You want sweetness, not dark brown onion.

Add the garlic, ginger and chillies. Cook for another minute or two, until the raw smell has gone.

### 4. Cook the tomato masala

Add the tomatoes, turmeric, ground coriander and chilli powder.

Cook for 7-8 minutes, stirring now and then, until the tomato has thickened and the oil starts to show at the edges.

This is the important bit. Raw tomato plus spices tastes thin. Cooked-down tomato masala tastes like dinner.

### 5. Add the mushrooms

Add the mushrooms and a good pinch of salt.

Turn the heat up and stir hard for 2-3 minutes, so the mushrooms are coated in the masala and start giving up their water.

### 6. Cover and cook

Reduce the heat to medium, cover the pan and cook for 8-10 minutes.

Stir once halfway through. The mushrooms will cook in their own liquid.

### 7. Reduce and finish

Take the lid off.

If it looks watery, turn the heat up and cook uncovered for a minute or two, until the masala clings to the mushrooms.

Stir in the garam masala and crushed kasuri methi. Cook for 1 final minute.

Finish with coriander and a proper squeeze of lemon.

Taste it before and after the lemon if you want proof. It lifts the mushrooms, sharpens the tomato and makes the spices feel brighter.

## Why this works

Mushrooms carry a lot of water. High heat at the start helps them release it quickly, then the uncovered finish drives it off so the sauce coats instead of pools.

The whole cumin and mustard seeds give the oil its base flavour. The tomato masala needs time to darken and thicken before the mushrooms go in, otherwise everything tastes raw and separate.

Kasuri methi goes in at the end because its flavour is volatile. Crush it between your fingers and it makes the whole pan smell like a restaurant.

The lemon at the end is what stops the bhaji tasting heavy. Mushrooms are earthy, tomato masala is deep, and the lemon cuts through both.

## No-faff notes

- Chestnut mushrooms have more flavour than white button mushrooms, but both work.
- Ghee is excellent here if you do not need the recipe to be vegan.
- If the mushrooms crowd the pan badly, cook them in a wide pan or they will steam forever.
- Do not skip the lemon. If the bhaji tastes good but slightly dull, this is what it is missing.
- Serve with [chapatti](/recipes/chapatti/), rice, dal or plain yoghurt.

## No-faff swaps

- No fresh tomatoes? Use about 200g chopped tomatoes or passata.
- No fresh ginger? Use a teaspoon of ginger paste, or leave it out.
- No mustard seeds? Use cumin seeds only. It will still be good.
- No kasuri methi? Leave it out and add extra coriander at the end.
- Want it wetter? Add a splash of water after the mushrooms have cooked down.
