This is the mushroom ragu to make when you want a proper lasagne without pretending mushrooms are mince.

It is deep, savoury and thick enough to spoon into layers. It also works with pappardelle if lasagne feels like a job for another day.

## Ingredients

### Mushrooms

- 30g dried porcini mushrooms
- 350ml boiling water
- 680g chestnut mushrooms, hand-chopped small
- 225g oyster or shiitake mushrooms, torn or roughly chopped

### Soffritto

- 3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil, plus more for frying mushrooms
- 1 large onion, finely diced
- 2 carrots, finely diced
- 2 celery sticks, finely diced
- Salt and black pepper

### Sauce

- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 tablespoons tomato puree
- 180ml dry white or red wine
- 1 x 800g tin crushed tomatoes
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce or tamari
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1/2 teaspoon chilli flakes, optional
- 1 bay leaf
- 80ml whole milk or cream
- 1 tablespoon balsamic vinegar
- Small handful of basil or parsley, chopped

## Method

### 1. Soak the porcini

Put the dried porcini in a heatproof bowl and pour over the boiling water.

Leave for at least 30 minutes.

Lift the mushrooms out carefully, leaving any grit behind. Squeeze them dry and chop finely.

Strain the soaking liquid through kitchen paper, a coffee filter or a very fine sieve. Keep it. This is not washing-up water; it is flavour.

### 2. Chop the fresh mushrooms

Wipe the fresh mushrooms clean.

Chop the chestnut mushrooms by hand into small pieces. Tear or roughly chop the oyster or shiitake mushrooms.

Do not use a food processor unless you enjoy mushroom paste. Uneven pieces are good here. They make the ragu feel more substantial.

### 3. Brown the mushrooms hard

Heat a large heavy pan over a high heat.

Add a little olive oil, then add half the fresh mushrooms.

Leave them alone for 2-3 minutes so they sear, then stir and keep cooking until their water has come out, evaporated, and the mushrooms are deeply browned.

This can take 10-12 minutes. Do not rush it.

Lift them into a bowl and repeat with the remaining fresh mushrooms, adding more oil if needed.

### 4. Cook the soffritto

Turn the heat down to medium.

Add the olive oil, onion, carrot and celery to the same pan with a good pinch of salt and plenty of black pepper.

Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the vegetables are very soft and the onion is just starting to turn golden.

### 5. Build the base

Add the garlic and chopped porcini. Cook for 1-2 minutes.

Add the tomato puree and stir it through the vegetables.

Cook for 2-3 minutes, until it darkens to a rusty red and stops smelling raw.

Pour in the wine, scraping the bottom of the pan as it bubbles. Simmer for 3-4 minutes, until reduced by about half.

### 6. Simmer

Add the strained porcini liquid, crushed tomatoes, soy sauce, oregano, chilli flakes if using, bay leaf and browned mushrooms.

Bring to a gentle simmer.

Partially cover and cook on a low heat for at least 45 minutes, or up to 2 hours if you have the time.

Stir now and then so it does not catch. Add a splash of water if it gets too thick before the flavour has developed.

### 7. Finish

Remove the bay leaf.

Stir in the milk or cream and the balsamic vinegar.

Simmer for another 5 minutes, then taste for salt, pepper and vinegar.

Stir in the basil or parsley just before using.

## Why this works

The porcini bring depth, and the strained soaking liquid carries that flavour through the whole sauce.

Fresh mushrooms only taste meaty if you brown them properly first. If they go straight into liquid, they steam and taste flat.

Soy sauce and tomato puree add savoury depth. Balsamic at the end sharpens the sauce. Milk or cream rounds it off so it feels like ragu rather than tomato mushrooms.

## No-faff notes

- This makes enough for a generous large lasagne, roughly 6-7 cups of sauce.
- Make it the day before if you can. It gets better in the fridge.
- The finished ragu should be thick and spoonable, not watery.
- Let it cool a little before building a lasagne. Hot loose sauce makes awkward layers.
- Excellent with pappardelle and Parmesan if you do not want to build a lasagne.

## No-faff swaps

- No porcini? Use more chestnut mushrooms and add a teaspoon of Marmite or miso.
- No oyster or shiitake mushrooms? Use all chestnut mushrooms.
- No red wine? White wine works. No wine at all? Use vegetable stock with a splash of balsamic.
- No milk or cream? Leave it out, or use a little butter at the end.
- Want it vegan? Skip the milk or use unsweetened plant cream.
